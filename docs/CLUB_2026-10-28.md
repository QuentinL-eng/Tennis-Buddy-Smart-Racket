# Club session, 28 Oct 2026: what the raw recordings show

15 raw-stream CSV exports from the app (`data/club-2026-10-28/`, kept local, not in git). They cover about 86 minutes,
from 19:15 to 21:22 Pacific. They were made before impact mode (firmware 1.5), so this is raw streaming on a real
court. IMU only: the CSV has no mic data, so the sweet-spot model can't be checked here.

Reproduce:

```bash
python3 tools/csv_session_stats.py data/club-2026-10-28
```

## Findings

**1. Only about 17% of the 1 kHz stream arrived (13–30% per recording).** Samples come in runs of 8 or 16 (one or
two packets), separated by gaps of 17–25 ms. Median throughput was 214 samples/s, the 10th percentile 64 and the
90th 338. That is about one 8-sample packet per connection interval, far below what the link should carry. Earlier
estimates of a quarter to a third lost were optimistic: on court it was closer to four fifths. This alone rules out
judging shot quality from raw-stream recordings, and it confirms the switch to impact mode.

**2. Impact-mode windows will take about 2 s to deliver.** A 448-sample window at the measured median throughput takes
about 2.1 s. In these sessions, 16% of consecutive high-g events came less than 1.8 s apart, and the 10th percentile
was 1.0 s. Impact mode still sends a hit notification for each of these, but a window can't start while the previous
one is still sending. So expect roughly 1 window in 6 to be missing during fast exchanges, unless throughput
improves. Worth logging on court: `windowBusy` drops, plus the connection interval the phone actually grants (the
status line already prints it).

**3. The gyro clipped at ±2000 dps on court, although the app asks for ±4000.** 8 of the 12 recordings that have data
show gyro samples flat-topped at exactly 34.87 rad/s, which is the int16 limit at 16.4 LSB/dps. At ±4000 dps
the ceiling would be 69.7 rad/s. So during those swings the IMU was in its ±2000 dps range. On paper the code path
looks right: the app writes `gyroRange 1` on connect, the firmware switches between packets and flags the packets,
and the app decodes the flag. So check this on the hardware next. Connect over USB serial during a session and
read the status line, which prints `2000` or `4000`. Possible causes: an older firmware on the racket that night,
or a reconnect path that skips characteristic discovery and so never resends the command. None of the received
samples within 150 ms of a detected hit were clipped. The clipped samples come from the fastest swings, most likely
serves and swings whose impact fell in a gap. Swing speed and spin on those shots will read low.

*Follow-up (firmware 1.6):* ST's driver changes the gyro full scale only with the gyro powered down. Our firmware
wrote CTRL6 with the gyro running, the most likely reason the switch didn't take effect. Firmware 1.6 switches
the range ST's way and checks the readback. It also boots at ±4000 and returns there on disconnect, so the range
no longer depends on the app's command arriving. Each recording now gets a `.link.json` with the gyro range and
packet counts, so the next session will show which range was really in use.

**4. Accel low-g saturates on every hard hit (expected).** ±16 g clips on most hits. The high-g channel covers them,
with a median event peak of 21 g, 90th percentile 85 g and a maximum of 233 g (vector). On the wire, high-g is
sent at the app's default ±200 g range, although the sensor reads ±320 g. One sample in the first recording hit
200.7 g on the face-normal axis, and two others peaked at 184–187 g. A hard serve or frame hit will clip at
200 g, so consider the 400 g wire range (`h3lisRange` 3), at the cost of half the resolution. That cost is
negligible in practice: the 400 g step is 0.195 g, while the sensor's noise at rest is 0.35 g RMS and the median
post-impact ring is 3.9 g. The 200 g limit comes from sending high-g as old H3LIS 12-bit counts, not from the
sensor, which reads ±320 g at 10.4 mg per step.

**5. Session hygiene.** 3 of the 15 files are empty (0–16 samples). Two recordings contain a racket reboot. One of
them, `T022106`, then has a 17-minute gap, so its duration is overstated. `T024705` has no `-racket` suffix and only
low motion (a table or bench test?).
